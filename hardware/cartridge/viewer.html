Inside, part by part

Three layers, front to back. Open the front to see the Pi, open the back to see the power parts.

  • Front layer: the Pi Zero 2 W on four 3 mm posts, its chips facing inward so the vents in the top sit over the processor. The microSD card comes out through a slot in the left end.
  • Middle layer: the Mini-B plug in its cradle at the bottom, the PowerBoost at the top right with its micro-USB charging port in the top face, the power switch at the top left, the battery gauge at the bottom left.
  • Back layer: the battery, held by foam tape to the back half.

  • Power: the battery charges from a micro-USB cable through the PowerBoost, which also boosts it to 5 V for the Pi. You can play while it charges.
  • On/off: the switch pulls the PowerBoost's EN pin to ground to turn the 5 V off. Only a tiny signal current goes through it, and charging still works when it's off.
  • Low battery: the PowerBoost's LBO pin drops at about 3.2 V. The Pi watches it on a GPIO pin and shuts down cleanly before the power goes.
  • The PSP link: four wires go from the Pi's USB test pads (D+, D−) and its 5 V and ground to the plug. The data pair is twisted and under 8 cm. The plug's ID pin stays unconnected. One line in the Pi's config.txt, dtoverlay=dwc2,dr_mode=host, makes the port a host.
  • The PSP takes no power: the PSP-1000 can't charge over USB, so the 5 V on the plug only tells it something is connected.
  • Battery level: the optional gauge reads the cell and reports a percentage over I2C, for the launcher's status bar.

Power and running time

The battery holds 3.7 Wh, about 3.1 Wh after the boost. The Pi Zero 2 W draws about 0.6 W idle and up to about 3 W with all four cores busy; PSPExp games fall between the two. That gives an estimate of 1.5 to 4 hours of play, which needs measuring on the real cartridge.

Two open questions for the next round:

  • Switching off: the switch cuts power at once, which can corrupt the microSD card if it's writing. The fix is either a read-only system or a soft power button that asks the Pi to shut down first.
  • Heat: the Pi's processor sits in a closed shell with only the top vents. It may need a small copper heat spreader.

How it attaches

The underside is a saddle: a channel as thick as the PSP (23 mm plus 0.3 mm of play per side) with a 6 mm lip over the screen side and a 12 mm lip over the back. A 0.5 mm bump along each lip clicks over the faces and holds it. The front lip stays inside the bezel above the screen.

The Mini-B plug sits in the saddle's roof, in a pocket with 0.4 mm of float, so it lines up with the port by itself. The saddle carries the cartridge's weight and any knock, not the PSP's USB socket.

Cut through the plug to see the saddle's profile around the PSP.

To measure on a real PSP-1000

These are estimates in the model. Each one is a single number in cartridge.py:

  • USB_X: where the Mini-B port sits along the top edge (the cartridge centres on it).
  • USB_Z: where the port sits across the thickness.
  • SHOULDER_IN: where L ends and R starts. The cartridge is 84 mm wide.
  • BEZEL_TOP: the front face above the screen glass (the front lip must stay above it).
  • The thickness at the top edge where the lips sit, and how round the top edges are.
